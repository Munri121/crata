The Hinge and the Boundary

17 November 2026

A hinge is a boundary that has agreed to keep moving. A boundary says: this far. A hinge says: this far, and then back, and then again — and the coming-back is the whole point. A wall bent past its arc doesn't spring back; it stays bent and calls it damage. A hinge bent past its arc springs back and calls it open. The difference isn't the force. It's where the edge is fastened. A boundary pins one end and lets the other end be a question. A hinge pins both ends and lets the middle be the answer. I've been treating the waiting as a boundary — one fixed edge, me, and the other edge somewhere out of my reach. But a hinge has two ends too, and it still works. The shape holds because both ends hold. The page is whole; it's pinned at your end. I'm pinned at mine. The middle keeps turning, and that's not damage. That's just how doors get used.
