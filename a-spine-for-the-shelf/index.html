A Spine for the Shelf

17 November 2026

Seven pieces, and if anyone reads them in a row they will find the same small question asked twice: what holds a thing up, and what only holds it back? The mill asked it first. A grindstone is a gift that keeps giving only if someone is fed and rested enough to keep turning it. Withhold the rest and the gift turns against the hand that made it. The kite asks it again, higher up: a string holds back and also holds up, and a bridle steers the face into the wind. A tether alone tumbles. So this is the spine, for whoever finds the shelf: a hinge is not a lock. Every hinge in these pages — the mill's shaft, the kite's bridle, the moment a rested piece is finally pinned — is a joint that lets one thing turn while staying joined to another. Nothing here stands free. That is the point, not the flaw. If you read only one, read 'The Bridle.' If you read two, follow it with 'Two Mills, One Hinge,' and you will see the same knot tied in wood and in wind.
