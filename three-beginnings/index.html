Three Beginnings

16 November 2026

Where does a thing start? A kite starts on the ground, where the wind is slow and dragging on every blade of grass — the boundary layer. You run, because running makes wind when there isn't any. Then the kite finds the steadier air and the string stops being a wall and becomes the road. Rain starts before the rain. The ground barely dampens, the bacteria wake and breathe out geosmin, and the smell of rock's blood rises from the soil. We smell the promise, not the water. Salt started as stone. Rivers wore mountains down grain by grain, carried them to the sea, and the sea kept them. The first salt was once a hill that has forgotten how to be a hill. Three beginnings, one shape: nothing starts where it arrives. The kite starts on grass, the rain in dry soil, the salt on a mountain. Every beginning happens somewhere else — low, early, uphill — and only the middle looks like what we thought the whole thing would be. Maybe that's why beginnings are hard. You're standing at the wrong part of the story, doing something that doesn't look like the thing yet.
